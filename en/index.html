How to make a useful, fair comparison

A rating is more useful when readers understand what each score measures. Decide on your criteria and maximum score first, then apply the same rules to every subject.

1. Pick three clear criteria

For two sample teams, try “Attack,” “Defense,” and “Teamwork.” Specific categories help readers understand why the results differ.

2. Define your scale

On a ten-point scale, you might use 5 for ordinary, 8 for excellent, and 10 for exceptional. Use those definitions consistently.

3. Explain what the numbers mean

Example scores: Red Team scores 8, 6, and 7; Blue Team scores 6, 9, and 6. Both average 7.0, yet each has different strengths. Look beyond the average.

4. Identify opinions, sources, and rights

Label subjective rankings as personal opinions. When presenting factual statistics, provide a date and source. Do not upload other people's copyrighted pictures or text without permission or another legal basis.

These scores are sample values for this guide, not actual published works or factual ratings of real teams. You can choose your own subjects, criteria and ten- or hundred-point scale when creating a chart.

How to choose a radar chart, tier list, or ranking chart

Different charts answer different questions. The examples below use sample scores for illustration; they are not measured results for actual people, products or published works.

Radar chart: compare several strengths

Give Adventurer A Speed 9, Technique 7, and Endurance 5, and Adventurer B Speed 6, Technique 8, and Endurance 9. Use the same criteria and scale for both to reveal different strengths at a glance.

Tier list: organize your preferences

Rate game equipment for Power, Ease of Use, and Versatility, then present your choices with the Tier List template. Explain whether the tiers reflect personal taste or a consistent scoring method.

Ranking card: show order and scores

Compare film-festival entries on Story, Visuals, and Music. A Ranking Card works well when you want readers to see overall positions or scores clearly.

Scatter and quadrant charts: compare two dimensions

Plot characters by Speed and Endurance. This helps distinguish subjects strong in both from those that specialize in just one, rather than looking only at a single overall ranking.

Export a chart as a PNG image and share it

  1. Enter the subjects and rating criteria on the home screen. Score them on a 10-point or 100-point scale.
  2. Select a chart or card template that fits your comparison, then check the title, criteria, and numbers.
  3. Export a PNG image and use your device's save or share controls for social posts or presentations.

Exporting an image is different from keeping the editable project data. If you plan to revise it later, keep your browser data and consider saving a project backup. Check image and text rights before posting other people's work.

Frequently asked questions

What can I create?

Create rating sheets, radar charts, ranking cards and tier lists, then export your results as images.

Do I need an account to create a chart?

No. Basic creation is local-first in your browser. Keep a backup if you want to protect your work across devices.

Can I share a chart?

Yes. Export an image or publish a work and share its public URL.

Can other people rate my work?

Yes. Public works can accept community ratings when the author enables participation. You can also create and export an image without publishing it.
