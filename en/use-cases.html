CASE 1 · BAR

A fictional café menu

Five invented drinks are given personal preference scores on the same 0–100 scale. A horizontal bar chart makes a single-criterion ranking readable.

What this helps you see

Sort five drinks at a glance。 Citrus Tea scores 92, versus 68 for Cloud Milk: a 24-point gap. These are invented preference ratings, not sales, reviews, or real café data.

DrinkPreference / 100
Citrus Tea92
Mint Cocoa84
Berry Latte78
Spice Chai73
Cloud Milk68

How to recreate it

Choose Bar Chart, add five drinks and their scores, then show horizontal bars with value labels, ordered from highest to lowest.

What not to infer

Unlike sales or measured popularity, personal scores are opinions rather than objective facts.

CASE 2 · QUADRANT

Choose between fictional walking routes

Plot four imaginary walking routes using Quietness and Access rather than turning everything into a single total rank.

What this helps you see

Balance calm surroundings and ease of access。 Hill Trail scores 92 for quietness but just 35 for access. City Walk is the opposite, at 40 and 95. The best choice depends on the goal.

RouteQuietnessAccess
Riverside8565
Hill Trail9235
City Walk4095
Garden Loop7380

How to recreate it

Select Quadrant and set Quietness as X, Access as Y. Plot four routes and split both dimensions at 50.

What not to infer

These are hypothetical routes; there are no real travel-time or safety measurements.

An eight-week fictional study diary

Track a made-up self-rating over eight weeks, including setbacks. A trend view answers a different question from a ranked scorecard.

What this helps you see

Read change over time, not rank。 W1 starts at 35 and W8 ends at 82, up 47 points. The series also dips in W3 and W7; the path matters, not just the endpoints.

CASE 4 · RANGE

Compare fictional weekly targets with results

Four made-up activity plans each have a target and a completion score. Range / Dumbbell reveals the size and direction of each gap.

What this helps you see

Find over- and under-target activities。 Cleaning moves from target 50 to done 90, a +40-point difference. Sleep goes from 85 to 70, a −15-point difference.

ActivityTarget / 100Done / 100
Reading7065

How to recreate it

Open Range / Dumbbell. Enter Target and Done for every activity, then enable differences and show both source scores.

What not to infer

These are illustrative scores, not real lifestyle or health measurements.

CASE 5 · STAT-CARD

A fictional adventurer stat card

A one-page profile for an original character, Mira, with six rated strengths. It is not based on another game or copyrighted character.

What this helps you see

Explain one character's profile in a card。 Speed leads at 88 while Tactics is lowest at 67. Overall 78 is the rounded mean of the six category scores (77.67), not an official power level.

StatScore / 100
Power84

How to recreate it

Use Stat Card, enter the character name and six stats, then keep images optional for a fully typographic result.

What not to infer

Mira is a fictional tutorial character with no third-party artwork.

Use the chart type that answers your question

For one score across several candidates, use bars. For two competing priorities, try Quadrant. For a sequence of weeks, use a line. To compare a plan with a result, use Range. For a single subject with several attributes, choose Stat Card. None of these fictional scores claims to represent measurements from a real business, person, location, or study.

These five chart exports are generated using the app's native Canvas rendering code. Images may be shared as samples, but an exported PNG is not an editable source sheet.
