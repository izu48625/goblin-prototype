Chart creation guide

Choose the right chart for your comparison

Do you want to reveal different strengths, compare a single metric, or show an overall ranking? One set of scores can communicate different things depending on the chart you choose.

Start with the question you want to answer

What to showChart typeWhat readers can see
Strengths and weaknesses across metricsRadar ChartDifferent shapes across shared criteria
Who scores highest on one metricBar Chart / Dot ChartDirect position or length comparisons
Overall order and scoresRanking CardRelative positions and numerical results
Broad preference groupsTier ListWho belongs in each tier
Balance between two measurementsScatter / QuadrantPositions along two axes

Use a bar or dot chart when readers need to compare one value precisely. Use a radar chart when the pattern across several criteria matters more than the rank. A tier list groups subjects rather than showing every numerical difference.

Character stat chart: compare sample abilities

Imagine two sample game characters scored out of ten on Attack, Defense and Speed. Character A scores 9, 4 and 8; Character B scores 6, 8 and 7. Both total 21 points, but A excels at Attack while B has stronger Defense. A radar chart makes the difference in shapes visible even when overall totals are tied. Try a Stat Card to emphasize a single character's attributes.

To make the comparison, enter A and B as subjects, add Attack, Defense and Speed as common criteria, and score both on the same scale. Choose Radar Chart from the visual tools. For an ordered score summary use Ranking Card; to organize broad preference groups use Tier List. Check permissions before adding photos or artwork from existing franchises.

Three ways to avoid misleading readers

  • Identify personal ratings as opinions, not objective research.
  • Use consistent score limits, criteria, and time periods when you compare subjects.
  • Check rights before including photos, logos, artwork or text, and do not present sample scores as real statistics.

Frequently asked questions

Is a radar chart useful for only one metric?

For a single metric, a bar or dot chart usually makes the differences clearer.

Is a tier list the same as a ranking?

No. Ranking cards communicate an order or numerical scores; a tier list groups several subjects into broader categories.

Do I need to publish my work to create an image?

No. Creating an image and publishing a rating work are separate actions.
